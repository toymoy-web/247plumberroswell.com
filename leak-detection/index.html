The Water Bill Jumped, or the Meter Moves With Every Tap Off

That is how most leak detection jobs start. There is no puddle and no stain, just a bill that makes no sense or a meter dial that keeps creeping while the house is silent. Sometimes it is a sound instead, a faint hiss in a wall at night, or a patch of floor that feels warm under bare feet.

Leak detection is the work of finding a hidden leak without tearing the house apart to look for it. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing does it across Roswell, in 30075 and 30076, and in nearby Dunwoody, with a sequence of tests that narrows the search from the whole property down to a spot you could cover with your hand.

The first step is always the same: work out whether the leak is on your side of the water meter at all.

Most leak detection calls are routine. A water bill that crept up over three months can wait for a scheduled visit. Water dripping through a ceiling, a meter spinning with the house shut down, or a wet spot spreading across a floor is an emergency leak, and those calls go out day and night.

Which utility owns your meter depends on where you live. Homes downtown, near Barrington Hall and Roswell Town Square, are more likely to be on the Roswell Water Utility, while East Roswell neighborhoods such as Horseshoe Bend, Martin's Landing and Willow Springs are more likely to be Fulton County customers. Your bill confirms it.

Emergency Leak in Roswell? Stop the Water, Then Find It

A hidden leak becomes an emergency when you can hear water running with every tap off, when a ceiling starts to stain or drip, or when the meter is racing. Do these things first, then call the emergency line so the leak can be pinpointed before anyone cuts drywall.

  1. Close the house main. If the meter stops, you have bought time and confirmed the leak is on your side.
  2. Turn off the water heater. Switch the gas control to off or flip the breaker so an empty tank is not heated.
  3. Catch what you can see. Buckets under a dripping ceiling, towels at the base of a wall, and photos of the stain.
  4. Shut off power near the water. When a wet ceiling or wall is close to outlets or a light fixture, switch that circuit off at the panel.

Write down the meter reading before and after you close the main. It tells the technician where to start. Call (770) 214-4545.

Two Water Providers, One Rule About the Meter

Roswell runs its own water system, but it serves only part of the city. According to the City's 2024 water quality report, the Roswell Water Utility serves roughly 18,500 residents, drawing mainly from Big Creek and a groundwater well, with supplemental water available from the Tom Lowe plant in Johns Creek on the Chattahoochee. The other 80 percent or so of Roswell gets water from Fulton County.

Either way, the meter is the dividing line. Fulton County's service lateral flyer puts leaks between the meter and the house on the owner, and leaks between the meter and the main on the County. If the meter test shows a leak but everything inside the house checks out, the buried service line becomes the prime suspect.

Your water bill names your provider. That matters for who you call if the leak turns out to be on the street side.

Ground microphones and wall sensors amplify the hiss a pressurized pipe makes where it is losing water, even through soil or a floor. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences, which makes hot-water leaks under floors stand out. For stubborn cases, a harmless tracer gas can be pushed into the line and detected where it escapes. A leak under the concrete floor itself is covered on our slab leak page.

What to Check Before You Call

Ten minutes of checking can save an hour of searching.

  • 1
    Do the meter test. With all water off, watch the leak indicator dial on the meter. Movement means a leak somewhere.
  • 2
    Dye-test every toilet. Food coloring in the tank, 15 minutes, no flushing. Color in the bowl is a flapper leak.
  • 3
    Shut off irrigation. Turn off the irrigation supply and repeat the meter test to rule it in or out.
  • 4
    Look under sinks and at the water heater. Check supply lines and the relief valve pipe for drips.
  • 5
    Walk the yard. Look for a green stripe, soft ground or standing water between the meter and the house.

Tell the dispatcher what you found. It often halves the search.

High Bills and Leak Alerts

The Bill Doubled or the Utility Says You Have a Leak

A large share of leak calls start with paper, not water. A bill that jumped from normal to two or three times higher, or a notice from the water provider about unusual use, sends people looking for a leak they cannot see. In Roswell that notice comes from either the Roswell Water Utility or Fulton County, depending on your address, and both bill you for everything that passes through your meter.

You can check the basics yourself before anyone comes out. EPA WaterSense suggests reading the meter, using no water for two hours, then reading it again. If the number moved, something is leaking. Next, close the house main and watch the meter's small leak indicator. If it still turns, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

The usual suspects are a running toilet, a leaking irrigation zone, a water heater relief valve that drips into a drain, or a pipe leaking somewhere under the floor. When the simple checks come up empty, that is when acoustic and thermal leak detection earns its fee. Ask for the price of the detection visit on its own, so you can decide on the repair separately.

Ceilings and Walls

Water Stains on a Ceiling or Wet Drywall at the Baseboard

A brown ring on the ceiling under an upstairs bathroom is one of the most common hidden-leak calls. The cause is usually one of four things: a supply line or valve inside the wall, a drain joint under the tub, a failed shower pan, or a toilet seal leaking each time it is flushed. Each one leaks on a different schedule, and that schedule is the best clue.

A leak that drips whether or not the bathroom is in use points to a pressurized supply line. One that shows up only after showers points to the pan, the drain or the grout. One that appears after flushing points to the wax seal or the closet flange. A careful technician tests each fixture separately, with moisture meters and thermal imaging, before opening the ceiling.

When the drywall does have to come out, the opening should be planned and small. Mark the stain, take photos, and avoid poking holes yourself. A clean, square opening is cheaper to patch than a ragged one.

Outdoors

Irrigation Lines, Hose Bibs and Pool Fill Lines

A leak that shows up only when the sprinklers run is an irrigation leak. Shut the irrigation system's own valve and repeat the meter test. If the leak disappears, the problem is in the irrigation zones or the backflow device, not the house plumbing. Soggy turf, a patch of unusually green grass or a bubbling head usually marks the spot.

Pool and spa owners sometimes call about water loss too. We can test the supply and fill plumbing that feeds the pool from the house. Leaks in a pool shell, skimmer or return lines are a pool specialist's work, and we will say so rather than guess.
