Commercial Plumbing

Plumbing for Roswell Businesses

Commercial plumbing covers the systems a business relies on: restrooms that get far more use than any house, kitchen drains and grease lines, and commercial water heaters. It also covers backflow assemblies, gas lines to cooking equipment and the pretreatment systems the county requires. Commercial Plumbing Emergency: Keeping a Roswell Business Open

For a business, a plumbing emergency costs money by the hour. A floor drain overflowing in a kitchen, a restroom out of service in an office, or water coming up in a parking lot all need a fast response. Do these things first, then call the emergency line.

  1. Find and close the right valve. Know where the building main and fixture stops are before you need them.
  2. Stop dishwashers and sinks feeding the line. In a restaurant, every running fixture adds to a floor drain backup.
  3. Close affected restrooms. A sign and a closed door keep customers away from an overflow.
  4. Call the property manager. In a multi-tenant building, the backup may start in another unit.

Fulton County's Grease and Pretreatment Rules

Most of Roswell's businesses discharge into Fulton County's sewer, and the County has specific rules for commercial waste. Its code requires food service businesses that prepare food commercially to provide an approved fats, oils and grease pretreatment system, such as a grease trap or interceptor. Vehicle service and car wash facilities need an oil separator or sand trap.

The County's Commercial Pretreatment program monitors and inspects these systems and requires a permit for facility operations. New or modified grease traps go through plan review, with sizing based on the County's sewer use ordinance, often tied to seating capacity.

For a restaurant owner, that means three things: a correctly sized trap, a pumping schedule that keeps it within limits, and records to show an inspector. Grease that gets past the trap still ends up coating the building's drain lines, which is where jetting the kitchen lines comes in.

The IPC, which Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is based on, also sets minimum restroom fixture counts by occupancy type and calls for grease interceptors where grease-producing fixtures drain. That means a kitchen remodel or change of use can trigger both a County review and code upgrades.

What to Do Before We Arrive at Your Business

These steps limit downtime and damage during a commercial plumbing emergency.

  • 1
    Know your shutoffs. Every manager on shift should know where the main and fixture shutoffs are.
  • 2
    Stop water use on backed-up drains. Close affected sinks and restrooms and post a sign.
  • 3
    Protect electrical equipment. Keep water away from panels, POS systems and kitchen equipment.
  • 4
    Have the trap records handy. For grease-related backups, the pumping log helps diagnose the cause.

    Name an approver. Tell the dispatcher who can approve the work and the price.

Leaks and Floor Drains

Floor Drains, Parking Lot Water and Leak Searches for Businesses

Commercial leak calls look different from residential ones. A water department notice about a leak at a shopping center, water surfacing in an apartment complex parking lot, or a floor drain that overflows every time the dish machine dumps all need someone who can trace buried lines across a larger site.

The search starts with the meters. A business with several meters or sub-meters can isolate one building or one line at a time, which shrinks the search quickly. Water rising through pavement often comes from a domestic service line, but some sites also have a separate fire service line. That line belongs to the fire protection contractor, and we will say so if the evidence points there.

Overflowing floor drains in kitchens usually trace back to grease buildup in the line to the interceptor, not the drain itself. Jetting the line and checking the trap baffles fixes most cases. Routine trap pumping is usually handled by a grease hauling company on a set schedule.
