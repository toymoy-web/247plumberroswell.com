Trenchless Pipe Lining

What Pipe Lining Is and Why Roswell Asks About It

Trenchless pipe lining, also called cured-in-place pipe or CIPP, rebuilds a damaged sewer line from the inside. A flexible tube soaked in resin is pulled or inverted into the old pipe, pressed against its walls and left to harden. What you end up with is one continuous pipe formed inside the old one, installed through a cleanout or a small access pit instead of a trench.

Roswell asks about it more than most suburbs its size. Pipe lining is searched here more often than sewer line repair itself, and the reason is visible from the street. Many lots in the 1970s and 1980s neighborhoods sit under mature oaks and pines whose roots have wrapped around the sewer lateral for decades. Owners would rather not trench through those root zones or break up a sloped driveway to reach a pipe.

Lining only works on the right pipe, though. The rest of this page covers how to tell, what the job looks like on a Roswell property, and where a liner is the wrong answer.

East Roswell's established neighborhoods, Horseshoe Bend, Willow Springs and Martin's Landing, combine mature trees with long laterals, which is exactly where lining earns its keep. Downtown, near Old Mill Park and the Roswell Mill, lining can also spare hardscape and old stone walls.

Local Conditions

Why Digging Is Harder on a Roswell Lot

Mature root zones

Cutting a trench through the roots of a 40-year-old oak can kill the tree. Lining leaves those roots alone, though any already inside the pipe get cut out first.

Red clay backfill

Piedmont clay is heavy and slow to settle. A refilled trench can keep sinking for months and leave a low stripe across the lawn.

Sloped driveways

Many laterals run under driveways poured after the pipe went in. Breaking and repouring concrete adds days and cost.

Hardscape on top

Walkways, retaining walls and irrigation lines sit above plenty of laterals. Lining skips most of that disturbance.

None of these make lining the automatic choice. They explain why it is worth checking before anyone prices a trench.

The Process

How a Liner Goes In, Start to Finish

  1. 1

    Camera and measure

    Record the line, mark defects, measure length and diameter.

  2. 2

    Clean the host pipe

    Cut roots and scour scale so the liner can bond.

  3. 3

    Install and cure

    Pull or invert the resin-soaked liner, inflate it, let it harden.

  4. 4

    Reopen and verify

    Reconnect branch lines and camera the finished pipe.

A typical residential lateral is lined in a day, and drains are often back in use that evening once the resin has cured. You get the before and after footage. The cleaning step matters more than it sounds. A liner installed over roots or heavy scale wrinkles, so the pipe is cleared first with hydro jetting or a root cutter.

Where Lining Falls Short

A liner slightly reduces the inside diameter of the pipe. On a 4-inch residential lateral the loss is small, and the smooth, jointless wall usually makes up for it in flow. On a line that was already undersized, it deserves a conversation before the work starts.

Lining does not fix slope. If part of the lateral has settled into a belly, the liner follows the sag and the low spot keeps collecting paper and solids. That section needs digging and re-bedding.

A liner also needs access. Some older Roswell houses have no outside cleanout at all, so adding one is often part of the job, and Fulton County's current design checklist calls for cleanouts at the edge of the right-of-way.

Finally, a liner fixes the pipe you own. If the camera shows the defect is in the County's main rather than your lateral, that repair belongs to Fulton County.

Homeowners insurance rarely covers a worn-out lateral, though separate service line coverage exists. Lining is planned work, so the estimate is free once the line has been camera-inspected.

Standards

The Standards Behind a Proper Liner

CIPP is an installation method with published standards, not a single product. ASTM F1216 covers rehabilitating existing pipe by inverting a resin-impregnated tube, and ASTM F1743 covers the pulled-in-place version. Ask which method and which resin will be used, how the cure is checked, and whether you will receive the post-lining footage.

The drain piping inside your house falls under Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which is adapted from the IPC. Lining the building sewer does not change those rules, but it can change where your cleanouts sit, and any new cleanout has to stay reachable for future maintenance.

Your water provider makes no difference here. Whether your bill comes from the Roswell Water Utility or Fulton County, the sewer is Fulton County's everywhere in Roswell, so the same lateral rules apply in 30075 and 30076.
