Sound Familiar?

A Soggy Strip in the Yard, or Weak Pressure at Every Tap

Water line problems rarely announce themselves inside the house. Instead there is a patch of lawn that stays green and soft through a dry spell, a meter that spins with the house shut down, or pressure that dropped gradually until every shower feels weak. Sometimes it is a sudden stop: no water at all while the neighbors have plenty.

Water line repair and replacement covers the buried service pipe that runs from your water meter to the point where it enters your house. That pipe belongs to you. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing repairs and replaces service lines across Roswell in 30075 and 30076.

This page is about the pipe outside the foundation. Pipe inside the walls is covered on our repiping page, and the split between the two matters because the methods, permits and costs are different.

Long, sloped front yards in West Roswell neighborhoods such as Wildwood Springs and Litchfield Hundred can mean longer service lines from the meter to the house, and more ground for a leak to hide in before it surfaces.

The Meter Is the Property Line for Water

Fulton County's service lateral flyer is direct about this. If the water service lateral between your home and the meter leaks or breaks, the repair is the owner's responsibility. If the leak is between the meter and the main, it is the County's. Many homeowners do not realize they own that buried pipe until they get the bill.

Roughly 80 percent of Roswell residents are Fulton County water customers, according to the City's 2024 water quality report, and the rest are served by the Roswell Water Utility. Either way, the practical rule is the same: the meter and everything on the street side of it belong to the provider, and everything on your side is yours.

Never dig in or operate valves inside the meter box. If the leak is on the utility side, report it to your provider. If it is on your side, it is a plumbing repair.

Methods

Repair or Replace, Trench or Trenchless

Spot repair

One leak on a line otherwise in good shape gets dug out and repaired at that point.

Open trench replacement

A narrow trench from meter to house with new pipe laid on bedding. Simple in open lawn.

Pulled or bored line

A new line pulled or bored between two pits, sparing driveways, walks and landscaping.

Regulator and valve update

A new line is a good time to replace an old main shutoff and pressure regulator at the house.

Georgia's plumbing code, based on the IPC, calls for a pressure-reducing valve when street pressure exceeds 80 psi, so the regulator gets checked on every service line job. Before any digging, Georgia 811 marks buried gas, power and communication lines. We also replace service lines in nearby Johns Creek, where Fulton County is the water provider throughout.
