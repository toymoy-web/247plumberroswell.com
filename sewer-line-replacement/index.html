Sewer Line Replacement

When Repair Stops Making Sense

Sewer line replacement means removing or abandoning the old lateral and installing new pipe from the house to the public main. It is the right call when the camera shows failure along most of the line rather than in one spot: a collapsed section plus several cracked joints, a long belly, Orangeburg pipe gone oval, or clay pipe with roots at nearly every joint.

At that point, patching one defect just moves the next backup a few feet down the line. Replacing the whole lateral resets the clock for decades.

In Roswell, the new line ties into a sewer main owned and operated by Fulton County, and that connection point is where most of the paperwork on a replacement lives. If there is a chance the old pipe can be saved, compare lining the existing pipe first.

Replacement cost on a Roswell lot depends heavily on terrain. Steep front yards in West Roswell neighborhoods such as Wildwood Springs and Wexford can mean deeper laterals and harder access, while flatter lots in parts of Martin's Landing are often simpler to trench or burst.

Methods

Open Trench or Pipe Bursting?

Both install brand-new pipe. They differ in how much ground gets opened and what they can correct.

Open trenchPipe bursting
How it worksDig along the route, remove the old pipe, lay new pipe on beddingA bursting head splits the old pipe while pulling new HDPE through the same path
DiggingFull-length trenchA pit at each end
Corrects sags and bad slopeYes, the new pipe is bedded to gradeOnly partly. The new pipe follows the old path
Best forBellied lines, route changes, open lawnStraight runs under driveways, trees and hardscape
Watch forRed clay backfill settles for monthsNearby utilities and very shallow lines

Many Roswell lots end up with a mix: bursting under the driveway and front walk, plus a short open section where the line has sagged. Either way, a final camera inspection confirms the new line is clean and running at grade.

Permits, the Main and the Street

Fulton County controls connections to its sewer system. Its published requirements for lateral connections note that a County lateral connection permit does not replace the approval needed from the city or a state agency to work inside the right-of-way. In Roswell, that means a replacement reaching the street involves both the County and the City of Roswell.

The County's current sewer design checklist calls for a 6-inch lateral from the main to the first cleanout at a minimum 1 percent slope, with the cleanout at the edge of the right-of-way or County easement on the owner's side. The same checklist asks that no structure sit within about 5 feet of a cleanout, which is worth remembering before a new deck or retaining wall goes in.

Inside the property line, the building sewer follows Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, adapted from the IPC. Its slope table calls for at least 1/8 inch of fall per foot on 3 to 6 inch pipe. A line that never met that slope is one of the clearest reasons to replace rather than line.

Before any excavation starts, Georgia 811 has to mark the buried gas, power and communication lines on the property.

Materials

Choosing the New Pipe

For open trench work, the usual choice is PVC sewer pipe, either SDR 35 or heavier Schedule 40, set on stone bedding and backfilled in layers. Fulton County's own construction notes call for crushed #57 stone bedding under PVC wastewater pipe, and the same approach holds up well in Roswell clay. For pipe bursting, the new line is usually HDPE, fused into one continuous length with no joints for roots to find.

Whatever pipe goes in, the best protection against the next failure is good bedding and correct slope. Red clay holds water and shifts as it wets and dries. A pipe laid on stone and graded properly tolerates that far better than one dropped into a bare clay trench.

We also replace laterals in nearby East Cobb, where the sewer system belongs to Cobb County rather than Fulton County and the permit steps differ.
