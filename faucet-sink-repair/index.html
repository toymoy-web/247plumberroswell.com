Faucet & Sink Repair

Small Leaks, Real Water Bills

Faucet and sink repair covers the tap itself, the supply lines and shutoff valves under it, the sink basin and its drain connections, and the outdoor hose bibs that take a beating every winter. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing handles faucets and sinks across Roswell in 30075 and 30076, from a single dripping bathroom tap to a full kitchen sink and faucet install.

A dripping faucet seems minor. It is also water paid for twice, once through the meter and once through the sewer charge on most Roswell bills. A leak under the sink is a bigger issue because it soaks the cabinet floor quietly until the particleboard swells.

Most faucet fixes are quick once the right part is on hand, which is why knowing the brand helps.

Original faucets and shutoff valves in 1970s and 1980s Roswell neighborhoods, such as Martin's Landing, Edenwilde and Brookfield, are now 35 to 50 years old. That is about when multi-turn stops seize and old supply tubes start to weep.

Before You Call: Three Quick Checks

These save time, and sometimes the visit.

  • 1
    Try the stops. Close both valves under the sink. If they will not close fully or start dripping, mention it, since they will likely need replacing.
  • 2
    Clean the aerator. Unscrew the tip of the spout and rinse it. Low flow at one faucet is often just grit caught here.
  • 3
    Find the brand. Look for a logo on the handle, spout or base, or the manual under the sink. It speeds up matching a cartridge.

Outdoor faucet split after a freeze? Close the house main or the hose bib's indoor shutoff first.

Fixtures, Flow Rates and Older Roswell Homes

Georgia's high-efficiency fixture rules followed the Georgia Water Stewardship Act of 2010. For new construction permitted after July 1, 2012, they cap bathroom faucets at under 1.5 gallons per minute and kitchen faucets at under 2.0 gallons per minute. Faucets sold today generally meet those limits out of the box.

In older homes, the fixture is rarely the only issue. Many 1970s and 1980s houses still have their original multi-turn shutoff valves and hard copper or plastic supply tubes. Those valves often freeze open after decades without use, which becomes a real problem the day a supply line bursts. Replacing them with quarter-turn stops and braided stainless lines during any faucet job is inexpensive insurance.

Outdoor faucets deserve their own attention. A standard hose bib with a hose left attached in a freeze can split inside the wall. A frost-free sillcock, sloped to drain, closes the valve well inside the heated space.
