Water Softeners

Start With the Honest Answer: Most Roswell Homes Have Soft Water

A water softener removes the calcium and magnesium that make water hard, swapping them for sodium or potassium through an ion exchange resin. In hard-water regions, that protects water heaters, fixtures and glassware from scale. In Roswell, the need is usually much smaller.

Public hardness data puts Roswell's tap water at about 1.5 grains per gallon, which counts as soft on the USGS scale. Fulton County water comes from the Chattahoochee River, and the Roswell Water Utility draws mainly from Big Creek. Neither is the kind of mineral-heavy groundwater that makes softeners standard equipment.

So Roswell 24/7 Plumbing's water softener work in 30075 and 30076 is mostly repairing systems already installed, servicing softeners on private wells, and installing new ones where a test shows a real hardness problem. If a test shows your water is already soft, we will tell you not to buy one.

Even in larger homes in Brookfield Country Club or Chimney Lakes, Roswell's soft city and county water rarely needs softening. Private wells, which are uncommon inside Roswell, are the exception.

What to Do Before Calling About Your Softener

These checks solve a surprising number of softener complaints.

  • 1
    Check the salt level. The brine tank should have salt above the water line.
  • 2
    Look for a salt bridge. Push a broom handle down through the salt. A hard crust with empty space below means the salt is not reaching the water.
  • 3
    Confirm it is not in bypass. The bypass valve behind the control head should be set to service, not bypass.
  • 4
    Check the clock and settings. A power outage can reset the regeneration schedule.
  • 5
    Test the water. A test strip tells you whether the treated water is actually soft.

If those all check out and the water still is not soft, the control valve, injector or resin is the next suspect.

Water Softener Questions

Most Roswell homes on city or county water do not. Public hardness data puts Roswell's water at roughly 1.5 grains per gallon, which is soft. Softeners make the most sense on private wells, in homes with a specific hardness problem confirmed by a test, or where an existing system is already in place and working.

Inexpensive test strips give a quick reading in grains per gallon or parts per million. Your water provider's annual water quality report also lists hardness or the minerals that make it up. For a well, a lab test is worth doing before buying any treatment equipment.

A salt bridge, a hard crust that forms across the brine tank and keeps salt from reaching the water, is the most common cause. Other causes include a stuck control valve, a clogged injector or a system set to bypass. Break up a bridge carefully with a broom handle and see if salt use resumes.

Resin often lasts many years but wears faster on chlorinated water and water with iron. If the system regenerates but the water is not soft, worn resin or a failing valve is likely.
