Drain Cleaning & Unclogging

When a Drain Stops Cold in Roswell

Drain cleaning is the work of removing whatever is blocking a waste pipe, from a hair plug in a bathroom sink to a root mass in the main sewer line. Once it is clear, wastewater flows freely again to the Fulton County sewer or to a septic tank. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing handles drain cleaning and unclogging across Roswell, GA, at any hour, including the calls that cannot wait until morning.

A single slow sink is an annoyance. Water rising in a lower-level shower when someone starts the washing machine upstairs is an emergency, because the next load of laundry will push sewage onto the floor. The difference is where the blockage sits, and the quickest way to find it is to notice which fixtures are affected.

Two patterns come up again and again here. Kitchen lines in homes built in the 1970s and 1980s collect decades of grease on the inside of the pipe. Main lines under older, tree-heavy lots in neighborhoods like Horseshoe Bend, Martin's Landing and Woodfield pick up roots at their joints. Each needs a different tool, which is why the plumber asks questions before choosing one.

Not every clog is an emergency. A slow bathroom sink can wait for a weekday. Sewage rising in a basement shower, a toilet that overflows every time someone runs the washer, or a restaurant floor drain backing up during service cannot. Those are emergency drain calls, and the line takes them around the clock.

Knowing where your outside cleanout is saves time. Clearing the main from outside keeps the mess out of the house, and Fulton County's sewer design checklist puts cleanouts at the edge of the right-of-way on newer lots. Plenty of older Roswell houses have only an indoor cleanout or none at all, which is worth correcting during any repair.

Inside the house, the IPC, the model code behind Georgia's plumbing rules, calls for cleanouts at the base of drain stacks and where the building drain changes direction sharply, so those are the first places the plumber looks for access.

Drain Emergency: Sewage Coming Up? Do This Before Help Arrives

  • 1
    Stop every source of water. No flushing, showers, dishwasher or laundry anywhere in the house. Each gallon you send has nowhere to go but up the lowest drain.
  • 2
    Skip the chemical openers. Caustic cleaners rarely reach a main-line clog, and they sit in the standing water the plumber then has to work in.
  • 3
    Keep people and pets away from the water. Sewage carries bacteria. Wear gloves and boots if you have to walk through it.
  • 4
    Check the outside cleanout if you can find it. Water standing inside the cleanout pipe means the blockage is downstream toward the street. A dry cleanout means it is inside the house. Tell the plumber which.
  • 5
    Photograph the affected rooms. Pictures help with insurance and with any cleanup company later.

Cables, Cameras and Water Jets: Matching the Tool to the Clog

Most household clogs clear with a cable machine. A rotating steel cable, fitted with a cutting head sized to the pipe, breaks up the blockage and pulls part of it back out. A 1-1/2 inch sink line takes a small drum machine. A 4-inch main line needs a heavier sectional machine fed through a cleanout.

When the same drain clogs again within a few weeks, the cable only punched a hole through the problem. That is the point where a camera goes in. It shows whether the line is coated with grease, invaded by roots, sagging or cracked, and a sewer camera inspection records the footage so you can watch it yourself.

Grease and heavy root growth usually call for water instead of steel. Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure hose with rear-facing nozzles that scour the full pipe wall. It is the wrong tool for a fragile cast iron line with visible cracks, which is one more reason the camera comes first on repeat clogs.

If the camera shows a broken or separated pipe, cleaning buys time but not a fix. The conversation then moves to repairing the damaged sewer line or lining it.

Snaking

Snaking, Cabling and Rodding Are the Same Job

People ask for a drain to be snaked, cabled or rodded, and they mean the same thing: a spinning steel cable fed into the pipe to cut through the clog. A small drum machine handles sinks and tubs. A larger sectional machine handles the main line from a cleanout. Hydro jetting is a separate tool for grease and roots that a cable only punches through.

Kitchen sinks are the most common single clog. Grease, coffee grounds and starchy food build up in the horizontal run behind the cabinet, and the clog often sits in the wall rather than under the sink. Clearing it from the right access point and checking the flow afterward keeps it from coming straight back.
