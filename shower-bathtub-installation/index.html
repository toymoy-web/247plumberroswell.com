Shower & Bathtub Installation

The Plumbing Behind a Bathroom Remodel

Shower and bathtub installation covers new tubs, shower valves and trim, tub-to-shower conversions, shower pans and drains, and the rough-in plumbing that sits behind the tile. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing installs and replaces showers and tubs across Roswell in 30075 and 30076, working either directly for the homeowner or alongside a remodeling contractor.

The plumbing is the part of a bathroom remodel that is hardest to change later. Once the tile is up, moving a valve two inches or fixing a drain that was pitched wrong means tearing it back out. Getting the rough-in right, and inspected, before the walls close is the whole game.

In Roswell, that inspection is not optional. The City of Roswell requires a permit before anyone alters or replaces a plumbing system, and bathroom remodels that move or replace valves and drains fall squarely under that rule.

Older houses near the historic district and in the 1970s and 1980s subdivisions often still have their original tubs and two-handle valves. Replacing the valve is the right moment to replace the supply lines behind it as well, especially if the house has polybutylene supply pipe.

Code Points That Decide the Design

A few rules shape almost every shower and tub project. The IPC, which Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is based on, requires shower and tub-shower valves to be pressure-balance, thermostatic or combination types, so a flushing toilet down the hall cannot turn the shower scalding or ice cold.

Georgia's high-efficiency fixture rules, which followed the Water Stewardship Act of 2010, cap showerheads at 2.5 gallons per minute at 60 psi. Multi-head or body-spray showers have to be designed with that in mind, and the water heater has to be sized for everything running at once, which sometimes means a larger water heater as part of the project.

Drain sizing and trap placement matter most in conversions. A tub drain and a shower drain are often different sizes, and a shower pan has to slope evenly to the drain with proper waterproofing underneath. These are exactly the things the City's rough-in inspection checks.

Buying fixtures before the rough-in is important. Valve bodies are brand specific, and the trim you want has to match the valve behind the wall. Nearby homes in Sandy Springs built in the 1960s and 1970s follow the same process, often with an older cast iron tub to remove first.
