Water Mitigation

The First Hours Decide How Much Gets Torn Out

Water mitigation is the emergency phase of water damage work. Its job is to stop the damage from spreading: get the standing water out, find where moisture has traveled, remove materials that cannot be saved, and set up equipment to dry the structure. Full restoration and rebuilding come after.

The clock matters. Drywall, subfloor and insulation keep absorbing water for hours, and Georgia's humidity slows natural drying. A room that could have been dried in place on day one can need its lower drywall and flooring replaced by day three. That difference is what mitigation is for.

What to Do Before the Mitigation Crew Arrives

These steps protect the house and the claim.

  • 1
    Shut off the water source. Close the main or the fixture shutoff if you can do it safely.
  • 2
    Stay out of standing water near electricity. If water reached outlets or the panel, keep clear and contact your electric utility.
  • 3
    Document first. Photograph and video the water, the source and damaged items before moving anything.
  • 4
    Lift and move. Put furniture legs on foil or blocks and move rugs, electronics and papers to a dry room.
  • 5
    Open up if it is dry outside. On a dry day, opening windows can help. On a humid Georgia day, keep them closed and leave it to the dehumidifiers.

Stay out of any water from a sewer backup or outdoor flooding, which should be treated as contaminated.

How Drying Is Measured, Not Guessed

Surfaces dry long before the inside of a wall or subfloor does. That is why mitigation relies on moisture meters and thermal imaging rather than touch. Readings are taken at the start, compared against dry reference points in unaffected parts of the house, and checked each day until wet materials reach their dry targets.

The equipment does the drying. Air movers push air across wet surfaces to speed evaporation, and dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into the house. In Roswell's humid summers, dehumidification is usually the limiting factor.

The industry reference for this work is the IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which covers assessment, drying and verification. It is worth asking any provider how they document readings against it. Homes in nearby Sandy Springs near the Chattahoochee face the same humidity challenges during drying.
