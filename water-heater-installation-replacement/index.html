Water Heater Installation

Replacing a Water Heater the Right Way in Roswell

Swapping out a water heater means pulling the old unit and choosing a new one sized to how your household uses hot water. The new unit is then connected to the gas, electrical, water and venting systems to code. In Roswell, it also means a City permit and inspection. The City's Community Development department states plainly that water heaters fall under the plumbing and gas codes and require permits and inspections, and that state licensing applies when a tradesperson does the job.

That rule protects you. The inspection checks the venting, the relief valve discharge, the gas connection and the pan and drain where required. When the job passes, the City issues a Certificate of Completion, and the City itself suggests homeowners ask for that document before making final payment.

Options

Four Kinds of Water Heater, Four Different Fits

Gas tank

The most common swap in Roswell homes with gas service. Quick recovery, familiar venting, lower upfront cost.

Electric tank

Simple to install where there is no gas. Slower recovery, and sizing matters more for busy households.

Tankless

Heats on demand and never runs out, but often needs a larger gas line and new venting. Covered on our tankless page.

Heat pump

Uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Needs airflow, a condensate drain and a bit more room.

Changing from one type to another is where most of the cost difference lives. A like-for-like tank swap is the simplest job. Switching fuel or going tankless adds gas, electrical or venting work.

Code Details That Matter on Install Day

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is adapted from the IPC. A few of its water heater provisions come up on almost every Roswell replacement.

  • Relief valve discharge. The T&P relief valve on top of the tank needs a full-size discharge pipe that ends where hot water can do no harm, never capped or threaded shut.
  • Drain pans. A tank installed where a leak would damage the building, such as an attic or an upstairs closet, needs a pan with a drain line to a safe spot.
  • Thermal expansion. If a regulator or backflow device keeps heated water from expanding back toward the street, the code calls for thermal expansion control, usually a small expansion tank on the cold line.
  • Venting and gas. Gas units need correctly sized, sloped and sealed venting and a gas connection with a sediment trap and shutoff.

An older installation that skipped any of these will be brought up to date as part of the replacement, and the inspection confirms it.

Install Day

How a Replacement Visit Runs

  1. 1

    Shut down and drain

    Gas or power off, water off, old tank drained.

  2. 2

    Remove and set

    Old unit out, new unit set on stand or pan.

  3. 3

    Connect

    Water, gas or power, venting and relief discharge.

  4. 4

    Test and inspect

    Fill, light or power up, leak check, then City inspection.

You will have hot water again the same day for a like-for-like swap in most cases. The City inspection may happen that day or be scheduled after, depending on the inspector's calendar. If your current tank turns out to need only a part, repairing the existing heater may be the better call.
