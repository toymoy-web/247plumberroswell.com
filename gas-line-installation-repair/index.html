What to Do If You Smell Gas

This comes first because it matters most. Natural gas has an odorant added so leaks smell like rotten eggs.

  • 1
    Get everyone out. Leave the building now, pets included, and leave doors open behind you.
  • 2
    Do not create a spark. No light switches, no phone calls inside, no lighters, no starting cars in an attached garage.
  • 3
    Call from outside. Dial 911 or Atlanta Gas Light's emergency line once you are well away from the house.
  • 4
    Do not go back in. Wait until the utility or fire department says it is safe.
  • 5
    Then call for the repair. Once the gas is shut off and the source is found, the house piping repair is a plumbing job.

The gas utility's job is making the scene safe. Repairing the pipe on your side of the meter is ours.

Gas Line Installation & Repair

House Gas Piping in Roswell

Gas line installation and repair covers the natural gas piping from the outlet of your meter to every appliance in and around the house. That includes water heaters, furnaces, ranges, dryers, fireplaces, grills, fire pits and standby generators. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing installs, extends and repairs that piping across Roswell in 30075 and 30076.

In metro Atlanta, Atlanta Gas Light owns the gas mains and the meter. Your gas marketer sends the bill. Everything downstream of the meter belongs to the property owner, which is why repairing it, testing it and extending it for a new appliance falls to a licensed professional hired by you.

Georgia builds its gas rules on the International Fuel Gas Code, and the City of Roswell requires a permit for gas work, with an inspection that includes a pressure test before any new piping is hidden in walls or floors.

CSST, Bonding and Georgia Thunderstorms

Many Roswell homes built or remodeled since the 1990s use corrugated stainless steel tubing, or CSST, for gas. It is flexible and quick to install, and it is a legitimate material. It also has a known weakness: a nearby lightning strike can arc through the thin wall and cause a leak or fire if the system is not bonded.

The International Fuel Gas Code requires CSST systems to be bonded to the home's electrical grounding electrode system. Older installations sometimes predate that requirement or were done without it. If your house has yellow- or black-jacketed flexible gas piping in the basement, crawl space or attic, it is worth confirming the bonding is in place.

Other code basics show up on every job: a sediment trap ahead of most appliances, an accessible shutoff valve at each one, and a pressure test before concealment.
