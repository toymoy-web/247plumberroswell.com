Garbage Disposal Repair

When the Disposal Stops, the Kitchen Stops

Garbage disposal repair and installation covers clearing jams, resetting tripped units, fixing leaks at the sink flange and drain connections, and replacing disposals that have reached the end of their life. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing works on disposals across Roswell in 30075 and 30076, usually alongside the kitchen drain the disposal feeds.

Many disposal problems can be solved in a few minutes without a visit, and the dispatcher will walk you through the safe ones on the phone. The ones that need a plumber are motor failures, leaks from the housing, and clogs that sit past the disposal in the kitchen line.

That last one is common in Roswell homes from the 1970s and 1980s, where decades of grease have narrowed the kitchen branch. A new disposal will not fix a drain that is already choked.

Kitchen branches in East Roswell homes built since the 1970s, in Willow Springs, Martin's Landing and the streets off Old Alabama Road, have had decades to collect grease, so a disposal swap is a good time to check the drain behind it.

What to Do Before You Call

Safe steps only. Keep your hands out of the unit.

  • 1
    Switch it off. Turn off the wall switch, and unplug it or turn off the breaker if you will be working underneath.
  • 2
    Press the reset button. On the bottom of most units. It pops out when the motor overheats.
  • 3
    Free a jam from below. If there is a hex socket in the center of the bottom, turn it back and forth with the matching wrench, then reset.
  • 4
    Check which drains are slow. If the other sink bowl or the dishwasher backs up too, the clog is in the drain, not the disposal.
  • 5
    Look for the leak point. Top, side connection or bottom. It decides between a repair and a replacement.

If the unit still hums or nothing happens after a reset, call.

Disposals are relatively inexpensive, so replacement makes sense sooner than with most fixtures.

If the sink drains slowly even with the disposal running fine, the blockage is further down, and clearing the kitchen drain or jetting out the grease is the real fix.

Grease, Disposals and Roswell Kitchen Lines

A disposal grinds food into small particles, but it does nothing about grease. Fat poured down a sink cools as it travels and coats the inside of the kitchen drain. Over years, that coating narrows the pipe until the disposal's output starts catching.

Kitchen branches in older Roswell homes often run long distances through crawl spaces or basements before reaching the main, which gives grease plenty of pipe to cling to. That is why a disposal replacement in an older house sometimes goes hand in hand with cleaning the kitchen line.

The best habit costs nothing: wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing them, and put fats in the trash.

Garbage Disposal Questions

Turn it off at the switch first. A humming disposal is usually jammed. Many units have a hex socket in the center of the bottom where an Allen wrench can turn the motor back and forth to free it, then the red reset button on the bottom can be pressed. Never put a hand inside the unit. If it still hums, the motor or a lodged object needs a service visit.

Where it leaks tells you why. At the top means the sink flange seal. At the side connections means the dishwasher hose or drain elbow. From the bottom of the housing usually means internal seals have failed, and that unit needs replacing.

Many homes on septic, which are more common in parts of nearby Milton than in Roswell, do run disposals, but food waste adds solids to the tank and can mean more frequent pumping. Use it lightly and ask your septic contractor about your tank size.

Grease and oil, coffee grounds in quantity, eggshells, fibrous peels and stalks, bones, rice and pasta in large amounts, and anything that is not food. Grease is the worst offender because it coats the drain line downstream long after the disposal has done its job.

Many units last around 8 to 15 years depending on quality and use. Rust on the housing, frequent jams and a leaking bottom are signs a replacement is close.

Yes. On a new disposal, the knockout plug for the dishwasher inlet has to be removed before connecting the hose, a step that is easy to miss and causes the dishwasher to back up. The IPC, which Georgia's plumbing code is based on, also requires that dishwasher drain line to either pass through an air gap or rise and fasten to the underside of the counter before it connects, so dirty water cannot siphon back into the dishwasher.

An air gap leaks when the hose from it to the disposal or drain is clogged. The usual cause is food debris in the disposal inlet or a kinked hose. Clearing the inlet and the hose usually solves it.
