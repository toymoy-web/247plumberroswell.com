Sewer Camera Inspection

What a Camera Inspection Tells You

A sewer camera inspection is a video survey of the inside of a drain or sewer line. A plumber feeds a waterproof camera head on a flexible push cable through a cleanout, a toilet flange or a roof vent, then watches the pipe on a monitor as the camera travels toward the street. The recording shows the pipe material, its condition joint by joint, and exactly where any problem sits.

For Roswell homeowners it answers the question every sewer quote depends on: is this a clog, or is the pipe failing? Lining, spot repairs and full replacements are all priced from what the camera shows. So is the decision to leave a sound line alone.

Timing

Good Times to Put a Camera in the Line

After a main-line backup

Something stopped the flow. The camera shows whether it was a one-time clog or a defect that will do it again.

When clogs keep returning

Two main-line clogs in a year is a pattern. Something in the pipe is catching waste.

Before buying a house

Standard home inspections skip the buried lateral. A camera run before closing can head off an expensive surprise.

Before and after a repair

Footage before shows what needs fixing. Footage after shows it was fixed.

If the inspection turns up damage, the next page to read is fixing a damaged sewer line. If it turns up only buildup, drain cleaning or jetting may be all the line needs.

The Visit

How the Inspection Works

  1. 1

    Find access

    An outside cleanout if there is one, otherwise a toilet flange or vent.

  2. 2

    Run the camera

    Push it toward the main while watching and recording.

  3. 3

    Locate defects

    A transmitter in the camera head lets a surface locator mark spot and depth.

  4. 4

    Review together

    Walk through the footage and the options before any quote.

The camera head carries a small sonde transmitter, commonly set to 512 Hz on plumbing locators. A receiver on the surface picks up that signal to show the exact spot and depth, so any later digging goes in the right place. A flooded or fully blocked line may need clearing first so the camera can see.

Common Defects and What They Mean

These are the findings that show up most in Roswell laterals, and what each one usually leads to.

DefectWhat it looks like on screenWhat it usually means
Root intrusionHair-like or matted roots at a jointThe joint is open. Roots return after cutting unless it is sealed or lined
Belly or sagThe camera dips into standing water that never drainsThat section settled. Lining will not fix slope
Offset jointA visible step where two pipe sections meetGround movement. The step snags paper and waste
Crack or fractureLines or breaks in the pipe wallStructural damage. Spot repair or liner, depending on extent
Scale and tuberculationRough, crusty growth on cast iron wallsOld cast iron narrowing. Descale, then line or replace
Deformed pipeAn oval or flattened cross-sectionOften Orangeburg. Plan on replacement

Municipal inspectors grade pipe defects with a system called PACP, published by NASSCO. Residential reports are usually less formal, but asking for each defect's distance from the access point, the way PACP reports do it, makes any quote easier to compare.

Buying an Older Home in Roswell? Scope the Sewer First

A sewer problem found after closing belongs to the buyer. Found before, it becomes a negotiation. That matters in Roswell because so much of the housing stock is 40 to 60 years old, with laterals running under trees that were saplings when the pipe went in.

For a 1950s or 1960s house near the historic district, find out whether the line is clay, cast iron or Orangeburg. For a 1978 to 1995 house in a subdivision like Horseshoe Bend or Brookfield Country Club, root intrusion at the joints is the main thing to look for. For newer construction, the camera run is mostly a quick confirmation.

Ask the seller whether the house has an outside cleanout. Without one, the inspection may go through a toilet flange, which takes a little longer. Houses in nearby Johns Creek were mostly built later, so a clean result there is more common, but any house older than about 30 years is worth checking.
