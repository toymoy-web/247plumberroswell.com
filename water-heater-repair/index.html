Water Heater Repair

No Hot Water, or Water Where It Should Not Be

Water heater repair means finding and fixing the part that stopped your tank from heating safely. That might be a gas control, a burner component, an electric element, a thermostat or a leaking valve. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing repairs gas and electric water heaters across Roswell, GA, in 30075 and 30076, and treats any actively leaking tank as an emergency at any hour.

Most calls start one of two ways. Either the hot water is gone or lukewarm, or there is water on the floor around the tank. The first is inconvenient. The second can soak a finished basement or a garage full of stored boxes in a few hours, and a tank installed in an attic or a closet over living space can bring a ceiling down.

A good repair visit answers three questions: what failed, whether the tank is worth fixing, and whether anything around it, like the venting or the pressure in your water line, caused the failure.

Leaking Tank? What to Do Before Help Arrives

These steps limit the damage while you wait. Skip any you cannot do safely.

  • 1
    Cut the heat source. On a gas tank, turn the gas control knob to Off. On an electric tank, switch off its breaker at the panel.
  • 2
    Close the cold supply valve. It is on the cold pipe entering the top of the tank. Closing it stops the tank from refilling as it leaks.
  • 3
    Keep clear of the relief pipe. Water from the temperature and pressure relief line can be scalding. Never cap or plug it.
  • 4
    Move stored items away. Garages and basements around tanks are often full of boxes that wick water up fast.
  • 5
    Note the label. Snap a photo of the rating plate with the brand, size and serial number. It speeds up any replacement quote.

Once the tank is off and the cold valve is closed, the leak slows to whatever is left in the tank. Tell the dispatcher which steps you managed.

Repair or Replace: An Honest Rule of Thumb

Standard tank water heaters commonly last somewhere around 8 to 12 years. Inside that window, most failures are parts, and parts are worth replacing. Near or past it, the math changes. A new gas valve on a 13-year-old tank buys time on a tank that may start leaking next year anyway.

Three findings make replacement the better call almost regardless of age: water seeping from the tank body, heavy rust at the base or the fittings, or a combustion problem that points to damage inside the burner chamber. Everything else is a judgment call you should get to make with the facts in front of you.

If replacement comes up, the options range from a like-for-like tank to a tankless unit or a heat pump model. Replacement also brings a permit into play, which repairs usually do not.

Around Roswell

Water Heaters in Roswell Homes

Where a tank sits in a Roswell house tends to follow the era. Many 1980s homes built on sloped lots put the water heater in a basement or a garage, where a leak is messy but contained. Some townhomes and newer houses tuck it into an attic or an upstairs closet, where a pan and a working drain line matter a great deal.

Roswell's water is on the soft side, around 1.5 grains per gallon in public hardness data, so the thick scale that shortens tank life in hard-water cities is less of a factor here. Sediment and anode wear still happen, which is why an occasional flush and an anode check on a tank older than five years are worth doing.
