Hydro Jetting or Snaking for a Root-Choked Roswell Sewer Lateral

How snaking and hydro jetting compare on root-choked laterals under Roswell's mature trees, why the camera comes first, and how to end the cycle.

By the Roswell 24/7 Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026. Updated October 12, 2026. About 4 minutes to read.

The Problem

Roots Are Back in the Sewer Line, Again

If you own an older house in Roswell under mature trees, there is a good chance you have met this problem: the main line backs up, a plumber cables it, the cable comes back wrapped in fine roots, and a year later it happens again. The question that follows is whether a different cleaning method would last longer.

The two main options are a cable machine, often called snaking, and hydro jetting. Both remove roots. They differ in how completely they clean the pipe, how they treat fragile pipe, and what they cost. Neither one closes the joints where the roots get in.

Why the Camera Comes First

Before choosing a method, look inside the pipe. A camera run shows whether the roots are entering at one joint or many, whether any section is cracked or collapsed, and what the pipe is made of. Those answers decide everything.

If the pipe is intact, hydro jetting usually gives a cleaner, longer-lasting result. If the camera shows broken sections, aggressive jetting can make things worse, and a gentler cable clearing buys time while a repair is planned. And if the camera shows roots at most of the joints, cleaning of either kind becomes maintenance on a pipe that needs sealing.

For a first clog in a line that has never given trouble, standard drain cleaning with a cable is often all that is needed, followed by a camera look if it comes back.

Long Term

Breaking the Root Cycle

Roots grow into sewer pipes because the joints are open and the pipe carries water and nutrients. Cleaning removes the roots but leaves the openings. That is why the cycle repeats.

The permanent options are a spot repair at a single bad joint, a cured-in-place liner that seals every joint along the run, or replacement of the lateral. On tree-heavy Roswell lots, lining is often preferred because it avoids trenching through the root zone of the same trees that caused the problem.

Until then, a regular maintenance schedule, timed before the spring and summer growing season, keeps backups from happening at the worst moment.

What to Do Before Choosing a Method

Ask these questions on your next root clog.

  • 1
    How old is the line and what is it made of? Clay, cast iron, Orangeburg or PVC changes the approach.
  • 2
    Has it been cameraed? If not, ask for a camera run after clearing.
  • 3
    How many joints have roots? One or two points to spot repair. Many point to lining.
  • 4
    Is any section broken or sagging? That limits jetting and lining both.
  • 5
    What does each option cost over three years? Repeated cleaning adds up, and a permanent fix may cost less over time.

The cheapest option in the first year is rarely the cheapest over several years. Adding up the expected number of cleanings against the price of a permanent fix makes the decision clearer.

Timing

When to Schedule Maintenance Cleaning

Roots grow actively through the warm months. Scheduling a cleaning in late winter, before spring growth takes off, clears the line when root mass is lower and gets ahead of the season when backups tend to cluster. A camera run at the same visit shows whether the root entry points are getting worse from year to year.

Keep the footage from each visit. Comparing them over time is the most honest way to decide when maintenance has stopped making sense and a permanent fix is due.

Maintenance

How Often a Root-Prone Lateral Needs Attention

There is no fixed schedule, because root growth depends on the tree, the season and how open the joints are. A useful rule is to track the interval between backups. If a line needs clearing every year or less, that pattern is telling you the joints are open and the roots will keep returning.

At that point the comparison is no longer cable versus jet. It is repeated cleaning versus sealing the pipe. Paying for clearing every twelve months adds up, and each backup risks water damage in a lower-level bathroom. A camera run after the next cleaning, with footage marked by distance, gives you the facts to compare the ongoing cost of cleaning against a one-time liner or repair.

Root Clog Questions
