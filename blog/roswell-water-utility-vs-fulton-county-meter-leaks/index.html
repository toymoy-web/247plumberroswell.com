Roswell Water Utility or Fulton County: Who to Call for a Leak at Your Meter

Roswell has two water providers and one rule about the meter. How to find out who serves you, test which side is leaking and call the right party.

By the Roswell 24/7 Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026. Updated October 12, 2026. About 4 minutes to read.

The Short Answer

Two Water Systems Inside One City

If water is bubbling up near your meter box in Roswell, the first call depends on which side of the meter the leak is on, and that depends on who supplies your water. Roswell is unusual here. The city runs its own water system, but it covers only part of Roswell. According to the City's 2024 water quality report, the Roswell Water Utility serves roughly 18,500 residents, and the remaining 80 percent or so of the city gets its water from Fulton County.

Both systems follow the same basic rule. The utility owns the meter and the pipe running from it out to the water main. You own the buried service line from the meter to your house and everything inside. Fulton County spells this out in its service lateral flyer, and it is the rule plumbers work from across Roswell.

So the practical steps are simple: find out which provider serves you, work out which side of the meter is leaking, and call the right party. This article walks through each step.

What to Do Before You Call Anyone: Find the Leaking Side

Ten minutes with the meter usually settles it.

  • 1
    Turn off every water use in the house. Faucets, washer, dishwasher, ice maker and irrigation.
  • 2
    Look at the meter's leak indicator. Most meters have a small triangle, star or dial that spins with even a tiny flow.
  • 3
    Close the house main and look again. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside the house or between the main shutoff and the fixtures.
  • 4
    If it keeps moving with the house main closed, water is escaping between the meter and the house, which is your service line.
  • 5
    If water is pooling at the meter box itself, especially on the street side, report it to the utility. Do not open the box or operate its valve.

Take a photo of the meter reading at each step. It helps the plumber and it helps any conversation about your bill later.

Utility Side

What Happens When the Leak Is the Utility's

If the water is coming from the meter, the meter box or the ground between the meter and the street, report it to your provider and let them repair it. That work is theirs by rule, and in most cases you should not pay a plumber to touch it. Fulton County's flyer is explicit that leaks between the meter and the main are the County's responsibility.

Utility repairs are scheduled by the utility, so a slow seep may wait a few days while a gushing break is usually handled quickly. If the leak is soaking your yard or threatening a basement, say so when you report it. Photos help.

A utility-side repair can also affect your house afterward. When crews shut down and restore a main, pressure swings and sediment can reach indoor fixtures. Running cold water for a few minutes and cleaning faucet aerators usually clears it.

Your Side

What Happens When the Leak Is Yours

If the meter test points to your service line, the repair is yours to arrange. Most Roswell service lines are buried a foot or two deep and run from the meter near the street to the point where the line enters the house. Older lines may be galvanized steel or polybutylene, both of which fail with age. Newer ones are usually copper or plastic.

The job starts by pinpointing the leak with acoustic equipment so only a small section of yard is opened. A single failure on otherwise good pipe is a spot repair. A line that has leaked before, or is made of a failure-prone material, is usually replaced end to end, sometimes by pulling new pipe through with little digging. Our water line repair and replacement page covers the options, and if the meter test suggests the leak is inside the house instead, hidden leak detection is the next step.

The same rules apply in Fulton County water cities next door. In Johns Creek, where Fulton's Tom Lowe treatment plant is located, the meter is the dividing line exactly as it is in Roswell.

A Few Habits Worth Building

Know where your meter box is and keep it clear of mulch and shrubs so the utility can reach it. Know where the house main is and make sure it turns. Write both providers' after-hours numbers on a label near the main shutoff.

And check the meter's leak indicator once or twice a year with everything off. A slow service line leak can run for months before it shows up as a soggy patch, and the bill is often the first clue.
