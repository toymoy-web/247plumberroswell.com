Planning a Polybutylene-to-PEX Repipe in a 1978 to 1995 Roswell Home

Found PB2110 pipe at the water heater? How a planned PEX repipe works room by room in a Roswell home, from layout choices to permits.

By the Roswell 24/7 Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026. Updated October 12, 2026. About 4 minutes to read.

Why Plan

A Repipe Goes Better When You Choose the Date

Polybutylene was installed in homes across metro Atlanta from roughly 1978 to 1995, including many Roswell subdivisions such as Horseshoe Bend, Martin's Landing, Brookfield Country Club and Edenwilde. It is flexible gray or sometimes blue plastic, often stamped PB2110, and it has a reputation for failing without much warning, usually at fittings or where it has been stressed.

The worst time to deal with it is after a fitting lets go above a finished ceiling. The best time is on a calendar you control. This article walks through how a planned polybutylene-to-PEX repipe works in a typical Roswell house, room by room.

What to Do Before You Get Quotes

Confirm what you have and gather the details that shape the plan.

  • 1
    Find the pipe. Look at the water heater, under sinks, behind toilets and at the main shutoff for gray plastic stamped PB2110.
  • 2
    Check the service line. If you can see where the line from the meter enters the house, note its material too.
  • 3
    Count fixtures. Bathrooms, kitchen, laundry, ice maker, hose bibs and any wet bar.
  • 4
    Note the foundation. Crawl space, basement or slab changes how pipe is routed.
  • 5
    Ask your insurer. Some insurers ask about polybutylene. Know what paperwork they want afterward.

Photos of the pipe at the water heater and under a sink are enough for a first conversation.

Design Choices

Manifold or Trunk-and-Branch?

PEX systems are usually laid out one of two ways. A trunk-and-branch layout runs a larger main line through the house with smaller branches to each fixture, much like the original piping. A manifold layout runs individual lines from a central manifold to each fixture, often with a shutoff for each line.

Manifolds make it easy to shut off one fixture without affecting the rest of the house, and they can deliver more consistent flow when several fixtures run at once. Trunk-and-branch uses less tubing and can be simpler to route in some homes. In many Roswell houses over crawl spaces or basements, either works well because most runs can go underneath.

Also decide on PEX type. PEX-A, PEX-B and PEX-C differ in how they are made and connected. Your plumber should explain which fittings they use and why.

Permits, Inspection and Paperwork

The City of Roswell requires a permit before anyone alters or replaces a plumbing system, and a repipe is inspected before walls are closed. Keep the final inspection documents with your closing papers. Future buyers and insurers may ask how the polybutylene question was resolved.

After

Living With the New System

Once the repipe is done, label the main shutoff and, if you have a manifold, label each line. Test the main valve once a year so it turns when you need it.

Keep the plumber's invoice, the permit number and the inspection record together. And check your water heater's age while you are at it. A repipe is a natural time to replace an aging tank or add an expansion tank if the system needs one.

Budgeting

Budgeting and Scheduling a Repipe

Repipe prices depend on the number of fixtures, the number of stories, the foundation type and how much wall and ceiling patching is included. A one-story house over a crawl space is typically the simplest. A two-story slab home with finished ceilings is the most involved.

Plan the dates around your household. Water is usually off during working hours and restored in the evening, so school and work days are often easier than weekends. Clear access under sinks, in closets that share walls with bathrooms, and to the crawl space or basement before the crew arrives.

Finally, set aside a small contingency. Opening walls in a 30-year-old house can turn up a previous repair in an odd material or a section of damaged framing. A good contractor will tell you about it before doing extra work.

After the Work

Keep the Paperwork From Your Repipe

When the repipe passes its final inspection, the City of Roswell issues a certificate showing the job was completed, and the City itself suggests homeowners get that document before making final payment. Keep it with the invoice, the material specs and any photos of the new lines.

That file does two jobs. It answers an insurer's question about polybutylene with proof that it is gone, and it answers a buyer's inspector when you sell. Note which lines, if any, were abandoned in place rather than removed, so no one later mistakes an old gray pipe in a wall for live plumbing. The new lines themselves are sized, supported and tested under Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which is adapted from the IPC, and the pressure test before walls close is the step the City inspector wants to see.
