Sound Familiar?

Hot Water Goes Cold Mid-Shower, or the Display Shows a Code

Those two complaints account for most tankless service calls, and people rarely search for "tankless service" when they happen. They search for the symptom. The shower runs hot, then cold, then hot again. Or the unit shuts off and flashes a two or three digit code nobody in the house understands.

Both usually trace back to a short list: a clogged inlet filter, scale on the heat exchanger, a gas line too small for the unit, a venting problem, or a flow rate too low to keep the burner lit. Tankless water heater service is the work of finding which one and fixing it, plus the routine descaling that keeps these units running.

Descaling in Soft-Water Roswell

Scale forms when minerals in heated water drop out onto hot metal. In hard-water regions, tankless heat exchangers can clog in a year or two without service. Roswell's water sits at roughly 1.5 grains per gallon in public hardness data, which counts as soft, so scale builds slowly here.

That is good news, not a license to skip maintenance. Most manufacturers still call for periodic descaling, often yearly, and many warranties depend on it. A descale runs a food-safe cleaning solution through the heat exchanger with a small pump for about an hour, using isolation valves on the inlet and outlet. Units installed without those valves are harder to service, and adding them is worth doing on the first visit.

The inlet filter screen needs attention too. Grit from a nearby water main repair can clog it faster than minerals ever will.

Before a Tank-to-Tankless Conversion

Most surprises in a conversion come from these five items. Check them before you commit.

  • 1
    Gas capacity. Large gas tankless units need far more fuel at full fire than a tank. The existing gas line and meter have to be able to supply it.
  • 2
    Venting route. Condensing models vent through PVC, often out a side wall. The route has to meet clearance rules from windows and doors.
  • 3
    Condensate drain. Condensing units produce acidic condensate that needs a drain, sometimes with a neutralizer.
  • 4
    Power. Even gas tankless units need a nearby outlet for controls and fans.
  • 5
    Permit. In Roswell, replacing a water heater requires a City permit and inspection, and a conversion is no exception.

If any of these look complicated, a standard tank or heat pump model may be the better value. The water heater repair page covers keeping an existing tank running instead.

Tankless Questions From Roswell

Write down the code and the model number from the label, then check whether the unit has power and gas. Many codes point to ignition, flame loss, venting or overheating, and some clear after a reset. If the code comes back, call with it. The plumber can often narrow the cause before arriving.

The most common causes are a flow rate below what the unit needs to stay lit, a dirty inlet filter, scale on the heat exchanger or a gas supply that cannot keep up. A mixing valve set too cold can cause it too. Cleaning the filter and a descale often solve it.

Manufacturers commonly suggest a descale about once a year, and some ask for more frequent service in hard water. Roswell's water is soft, so many homes can follow the manual's standard interval without problems. Follow your unit's manual, since skipping required maintenance can affect the warranty.

Usually, but check three things first. Gas tankless units need a larger gas supply than most tanks, often more than the existing line provides. They need their own venting, commonly PVC for condensing models. And they need an electrical outlet for controls. A site visit answers all three.

They never run out the way a tank does, but they have a flow limit. Run too many fixtures at once and the temperature drops. Sizing the unit for your busiest hour prevents it.

That comes down to how much hot water you use and how many years you expect to live in the house. The energy savings add up slowly in low-use households, and conversion costs are real. For a straight comparison, see the options on our water heater replacement page.
