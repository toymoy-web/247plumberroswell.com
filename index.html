A 24/7 Plumber Who Knows Which Pipe Is Yours

An emergency plumber is the person you call when water, sewage or gas is going somewhere it should not. Waiting until morning would only make the damage worse. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing takes that call around the clock for homes and businesses in Roswell, Georgia, across ZIP codes 30075 and 30076, from the old mill-village streets above Vickery Creek to the subdivisions strung along Holcomb Bridge Road.

This is Roswell in Fulton County, north of Atlanta, not Roswell, New Mexico. The difference shows up in the pipes. Houses here sit on Piedmont red clay, the water comes from one of two different providers, and the housing stock runs from 1800s landmarks near Bulloch Hall to 1980s cul-de-sacs east of GA-400. Each of those facts changes how a leak gets found and how a repair gets priced.

Most emergency calls fall into a short list. A sewer line backs up into a lower-level shower. A water heater leaks across the garage floor. A supply line splits behind a wall, or the kitchen drain stops cold the night before a holiday. We handle each one in the same order: stop the damage, find the real cause, then give you a price to approve before the repair starts.

Who Owns Which Pipe at a Roswell Home

Half the confusion in a plumbing emergency is knowing whose problem it is. Here is how responsibility splits along the path water takes in and out of a typical Roswell house.

Owner.

Inside the house.

Fixtures, supply lines, the water heater and every drain. This is everyday plumbing repair.

Owner.

Meter to the house.

The buried service line from the meter box to your foundation. A meter that keeps turning with every tap closed often points here.

Utility.

Meter to the main.

The meter and the pipe out to the water main belong to your provider. Report leaks on this side to them.

Owner.

Sewer lateral to the main.

Fulton County holds owners responsible even when the clog or break sits in the right-of-way or an easement.

Which water provider serves your address?

Roswell runs its own water system, but it only reaches part of the city. The Roswell Water Utility serves roughly 5,600 customer accounts, mostly in and around downtown, from a treatment plant on the Big Creek watershed rated for 3.3 million gallons a day. Everyone else in Roswell gets water from Fulton County, and Fulton County runs all of the sewer lines citywide.

Your bill names the provider. Keep its number with your main shutoff key. If the leak turns out to be on your side of the meter, our water line repair team takes it from there.

Housing Eras

What Usually Fails, by the Age of Your Roswell House

The year a house was built tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. Roswell has three distinct eras, and each one breaks in its own way.

Era and where.Typical piping.What tends to fail.Usual fix path.
Historic Roswell (Canton Street, Mimosa Boulevard, Bulloch Hall area).Cast iron drains, galvanized steel supply, some clay laterals.Rusty water and low pressure from corroded galvanized, cracked cast iron stacks.Replace galvanized in sections, line drains that still hold shape.
1978 to 1995 subdivisions (Horseshoe Bend, Martin's Landing, Brookfield Country Club, Edenwilde).Polybutylene supply lines, often gray and stamped PB2110.Sudden cracks at fittings, root entry in laterals under mature trees.Planned PEX repipe, camera check of the lateral.
Late 1990s and newer construction.Copper or CPVC supply, PVC drains, some PEX.Pressure regulator wear, fixture valves, water heaters reaching end of life.Repair first, replace components as they age.

If your house sits in that middle row, the most useful thing you can do this week is look at the pipe coming off the top of the water heater. Gray plastic stamped PB2110 means the supply system is on borrowed time, and a planned repipe with PEX costs far less than a repipe done after a ceiling comes down. In the historic core, the priority flips to drains. A camera inspection of the sewer lateral tells you whether an old cast iron or clay line can be lined or has to be replaced.

In an Emergency: What to Shut Off Before We Get There

  • 1
    Close the house main. In most Roswell homes it sits where the water line enters: a basement wall, just inside the crawl space door, or in the garage. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • 2
    Leave the street equipment alone. If the house valve will not close, do not open the meter box or turn valves at the curb. That equipment belongs to the utility, so call its after-hours line.
  • 3
    Kill the water heater. For a leaking tank, set the gas control to Off or flip its breaker, then close the cold inlet valve on top.
  • 4
    Stay away from wet electrical. Keep clear of standing water near outlets, appliances or the panel. If water reached the panel, leave it alone and call your electric utility.
  • 5
    Stop all water use during a sewer backup. Every flush, shower or laundry load adds to what comes up the lowest drain.
  • 6
    Smell gas? Get out first. Leave the house, then call 911 or Atlanta Gas Light from outside. Do not flip switches or start a car in the garage.
  • 7
    Photograph everything. Take pictures of the leak, the damage and the failed part before cleanup. Insurers usually ask for them.
