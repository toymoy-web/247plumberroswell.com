About Roswell 24/7 Plumbing in Roswell, GA

Roswell 24/7 Plumbing is a phone-first plumbing service for Roswell, Georgia, and the North Fulton towns around it. The idea is simple. When a pipe fails, you should be able to reach someone at any hour, get honest answers about what is wrong, and hear a price before anyone starts cutting, digging or replacing. This page explains how that works, what we cover and what we do not.

Why a Roswell-Only Focus

Plumbing advice that works in one metro can be wrong a few miles away. Roswell has a split water system, with the city's own Roswell Water Utility serving part of town and Fulton County serving the rest. Fulton County also owns and runs every public sewer main. That split decides who you call for a leak at the meter and who pays for a sewer lateral that breaks under the street.

The housing stock matters just as much. Historic homes near Canton Street and Bulloch Hall still have cast iron drains and galvanized supply lines. Subdivisions built between about 1978 and 1995, like Horseshoe Bend and Martin's Landing, often went up with polybutylene supply pipe. Mature trees on those lots push roots into older sewer laterals. A plumber who knows those patterns asks better questions on the phone and brings the right equipment the first time.

How a Call Works

You call the number on this site at any hour. You will be asked what you see, where the water or sewage is, and what you have shut off. Those answers decide whether this is an emergency visit tonight or a scheduled visit tomorrow, and what the plumber brings.

On site, the plumber diagnoses the cause. For drains that means a cable and, when the clog keeps coming back, a sewer camera. For hidden leaks it means a meter test first, then acoustic listening or thermal imaging. You get an explanation of the options and an upfront price. Nothing starts until you approve it. Planned work, like a repipe or a water heater upgrade, gets a free estimate.

A quick disclosure, because you deserve to know it. This website connects callers with an independent local plumbing provider. Calls may be recorded for quality. Before any work starts, you are always free to ask for the provider's Georgia license number and proof of insurance, and you should.

Standards the Work Follows

Georgia writes its own minimum plumbing code, adapted from the IPC (the model International Plumbing Code) plus changes specific to this state. Every plumber who works here needs a state license, issued by the plumbers' division of Georgia's construction licensing board under the Secretary of State. There are two master levels. Class I covers single-family homes and smaller commercial buildings, while Class II carries no size limit. Any license number can be checked online in a minute or two.

Permits and inspections apply to certain jobs, including many water heater replacements, gas line work and sewer replacements. When a job needs one, you will hear that up front along with the price.

What We Handle

The core of the work is residential plumbing repair and replacement: drains and sewer lines, water heaters (tank, tankless and heat pump), hidden leaks and slab leaks, burst pipes, repiping, water service lines, fixtures, gas lines, backflow devices and water treatment. Light commercial work is covered too, from restaurant grease lines to office restrooms. The full list of plumbing services links to a detailed page for each one.

What We Do Not Do

Being clear about limits saves you time.

  • We do not repair utility-owned equipment. The water meter and the pipe from the meter to the main belong to your water provider, and public sewer mains belong to Fulton County.
  • We do not design or permit septic systems. Septic design goes to a licensed designer and the county health department.
  • Structural drying and water cleanup after a flood is a separate service with its own phone line, explained on the water damage restoration page. The plumbing side fixes the pipe that caused it.
  • We do not make health claims about mold or water quality. Questions about symptoms belong with a physician, and lab testing of drinking water belongs with a certified lab.

What You Will Not See on This Site

No invented reviews, star ratings, customer counts or award badges. No fake technician bios. No prices that change once the truck arrives. What is on this site is either publicly checkable (utility rules, licensing, local geography) or a plain description of how the service works. If something here turns out to be wrong or out of date, email contact@247plumberroswell.com and it will be corrected.
