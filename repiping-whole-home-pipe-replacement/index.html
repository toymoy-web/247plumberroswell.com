Sound Familiar?

A Stain Under the Bathroom, Rusty Hot Water or Pressure That Keeps Fading

People rarely start out searching for a repipe. They start with a water stain spreading across the ceiling below an upstairs bath, a second leak in a year, brown water in the tub when the hot tap first opens, or a shower that has slowly lost its pressure. Each of those can be a one-off. Together, or in an older house, they usually point to a supply system that is wearing out everywhere at once.

Repiping means replacing the water supply lines throughout the house, from where the service line enters to every fixture, with new pipe. It does not touch the drains or the sewer. Materials

PEX, Copper or CPVC for the New Lines

PEX

Flexible tubing with few hidden fittings, tolerant of freezing, and usually the lowest installed cost. The common choice for fished runs inside finished walls.

Copper

Rigid, long-lived and familiar. Needs more fittings and more wall access, and costs more in materials and labor.

CPVC

Rigid plastic used in some Georgia homes. Less common for full repipes today because it becomes brittle with age and heat.

Mixed systems

Copper at the water heater and exposed runs with PEX through walls is a common, sensible combination.

Whatever material goes in, Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, adapted from the IPC, sets the support spacing, sizing and fitting rules, and the City inspection checks them.

What to Do Before the Repipe Starts

A little preparation shortens the job and protects the house while the old pipe is still in service.

  • 1
    Find and test the main shutoff. Old polybutylene can fail any day until it is replaced. Make sure everyone in the house can close the main quickly.
  • 2
    Check your insurer's position. Some insurers ask about polybutylene. Knowing their requirements now tells you what paperwork to keep afterward.
  • 3
    Photograph existing stains. Pictures of old ceiling or cabinet stains separate prior damage from anything new.
  • 4
    Clear access points. Empty sink cabinets, clear a path to the crawl space or basement, and move furniture from walls near bathrooms.
  • 5
    Plan around water-off hours. Water is usually off during the workday and back on each evening. Plan laundry and showers around it.

If a fitting fails before the repipe date, close the main and call. That repair is treated as an emergency.

Routing

Running New Lines Through the Attic, Crawl Space or Basement

One of the first repipe questions is where the new pipe will go. In homes with a basement or crawl space, new lines usually run underneath and come up through the floor. In slab homes, or where floors are finished, lines often run through the attic and drop down inside walls.

Attic runs need insulation and careful placement, since an unheated attic can drop below freezing during a cold snap. Ask the plumber to show the planned route and the number of wall openings before work starts.
