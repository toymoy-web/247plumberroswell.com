Burst Pipe Repair

Water Pouring From a Wall or Ceiling

A burst pipe is a pressurized supply line that has split, cracked or pulled apart at a fitting, and until the water is shut off it keeps running at full household pressure. Burst pipe repair means stopping that flow, finding the break, replacing the damaged section and checking why it failed. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing handles burst pipes across Roswell, GA, in 30075 and 30076, around the clock.

Roswell has seen what a hard freeze does. During the December 2022 holiday freeze, Atlanta News First reported that Roswell fire crews were overwhelmed with burst pipe calls, logging more than 100 in a single day. Cold snaps here are short, which is exactly why homes are not always ready for them.

Not every burst happens in winter, though. Aging polybutylene fittings, corroded galvanized pipe and runaway water pressure cause splits in July too.

A burst pipe is a true plumbing emergency because the water does not stop on its own. Every minute adds gallons to the ceiling, the subfloor or the crawl space. The Roswell emergency line is answered at 2 a.m. the same way it is at noon, and the first question is always whether the main is closed.

Exposed pipe is where freezes do their damage. In Roswell that usually means crawl spaces under older homes near Barrington Hall and Mimosa Boulevard, and outside walls, attics and garages in East Roswell subdivisions such as Martin's Landing and Horseshoe Bend.

Burst Pipe Emergency: What to Do the Moment It Happens

Do these before anything else. The order matters.

  • 1
    Close the main shutoff. Clockwise until it stops. In Roswell homes it is usually in the basement, crawl space entry or garage where the line comes in.
  • 2
    Turn off the water heater. Gas knob to Off or its breaker off, so an emptying tank does not overheat.
  • 3
    Keep clear of electrical. If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of that area and call your electric utility.
  • 4
    Drain the lines. Open the lowest faucet in the house to relieve pressure and empty what is left in the pipes.
  • 5
    Document, then protect. Photograph the damage, then move furniture, rugs and valuables out of the wet area.

If the house valve will not close, leave the meter box alone and call your water provider's after-hours line. The meter belongs to the utility.

The Repair, and the Check That Comes After It

Once the water is off, the plumber opens only as much wall, ceiling or crawl space as needed to reach the break. The plumber removes the split length of pipe and splices in new PEX or copper joined to the existing pipe with fittings suited to its material. The system is then brought back up to pressure slowly and checked for leaks.

Then comes the step that prevents a repeat: checking static water pressure at a hose bib or laundry connection. Georgia's plumbing code, based on the IPC, calls for a pressure-reducing valve when street pressure exceeds 80 psi. A regulator that has failed open lets pressure climb and pushes the weakest fitting in the house until it gives.

Finally, the plumber looks for the next weak spot. A burst on one polybutylene fitting often means others nearby are just as old. We also handle burst pipes in nearby Milton, where larger homes with long runs to exterior walls see more freeze-related splits.

By Pipe Type

Cracked and Broken Pipes: What Each Material Tells Us

When someone calls about a cracked pipe, the first follow-up question is what the pipe is made of. Roswell houses built across five decades use very different materials, and each one fails in its own way.

Copper usually fails at a joint or through a pinhole. A frozen copper line often splits lengthwise in a short bulge. Polybutylene, the gray plastic used in many homes built from 1978 to 1995, tends to fail at its fittings rather than in the middle of a run.

CPVC becomes brittle with age and can crack near the water heater where it runs hottest. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside and gives way at threaded joints. PEX resists freezing better than rigid pipe, but its crimp and clamp fittings can still let go.

That matters for the repair. A single copper split can be cut out and replaced. A third failed polybutylene fitting in a year, or brittle CPVC that cracks every time it is touched, is a sign the rest of the system is close behind.
