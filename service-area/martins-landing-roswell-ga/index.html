Martin's Landing

A Lake Community Along the River

Martin's Landing covers about 1,000 acres in Roswell, along the Chattahoochee River off Riverside Road and Martin Road. Martin Lake sits at its center, with a trail around the shore. Neighborhood guides count roughly 1,600 homes, and some listings put the total near 2,000.

Most homes went up in the 1970s and 1980s. The mix is wider than in most Roswell subdivisions: colonials, split-levels and ranches sit alongside townhouses, condos and patio homes. Residents share pools, tennis courts, a dog park and a river clubhouse.

The US Census Bureau's address lookup puts Northshore Drive inside the City of Roswell. Holcomb Bridge Road is the nearest major road, and GA-400 runs just outside the community.

Plumbing When Homes Share Walls and Ceilings

Attached homes change how a plumbing problem plays out. A leak in one townhome or condo can show up in the unit next door or below, and the source is not always where the water appears. Testing each fixture on its own is how the true source gets found.

Responsibility can also be split. Your association documents usually say which pipes belong to the owner and which belong to the association. Read them, or ask the management office, before a large repair so the right party pays for it.

Water heaters in closets matter most when another home sits below. The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Georgia adopts with amendments, calls for a drain pan under a water heater installed where a leak would cause damage. If your closet heater has no pan, ask about one at the next replacement.

Lower levels near water deserve attention too. If your home has a sump pit, test the pump before storm season. You can look up your address on FEMA's flood map to see how it is mapped. Like most of East Roswell, Martin's Landing is more likely to be on Fulton County water. Check the provider name on your bill.

Before You Call From Martin's Landing

  • 1
    Tell your neighbor if water is moving. In attached homes, the leak may start next door or upstairs.
  • 2
    Find your unit's shutoff. In some townhomes and condos it sits in a closet, under a sink or near the water heater.
  • 3
    Check your association rules. Note who handles pipes inside walls and shared lines before a big repair.
  • 4
    Look at the sump pit after heavy rain. If water is rising and the pump is silent, call before it reaches the floor.
