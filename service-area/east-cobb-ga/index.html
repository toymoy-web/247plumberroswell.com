East Cobb, GA

Every Season Brings Its Own Plumbing Problem Here

East Cobb sits across Willeo Creek and the Chattahoochee from Roswell, in unincorporated Cobb County, and it gets both water and sewer service from Cobb County's own utility instead of Fulton's. The neighborhoods took shape quickly in the 1970s. Public histories of the county credit Ernest Barrett, who chaired the Cobb County Commission from 1965 to 1984, with pushing through the water lines and sewers that made that growth possible.

That history explains a lot of what fails today. Sewer laterals and supply lines from that building wave are now 45 to 55 years old, and the trees planted alongside them are fully grown. Problems come in a steady seasonal rhythm, which is how this page is organized.

Mature Trees Over 1970s Sewer Laterals

East Cobb's wooded character is part of its appeal, and it is also one of the most frequent reasons its sewer lines back up, according to local plumbing contractors who work there. Oaks and pines planted or preserved when subdivisions along Johnson Ferry Road, Lower Roswell Road and Paper Mill Road were built have had four to five decades to reach the laterals running beneath them. Clay and older joints give roots an easy way in.

Clearing roots with a cable or water jet works, but the roots return through the same open joints, often within a year or two. A camera inspection shows how many joints are affected. A few bad joints may justify a spot repair. Many bad joints on a pipe that is still round usually point to trenchless lining, which seals every joint without trenching through the root zone.

The same homes also carry original supply piping. A house with repeated leaks in different places is often a candidate for a full repipe.

What to Do Before We Arrive in East Cobb

Steps that limit damage while you wait.

  • 1
    Close the house main. It is usually where the water line enters the basement, crawl space or garage.
  • 2
    Stop water use during a backup. Every flush adds to what comes up the lowest drain.
  • 3
    Check the street. If neighbors are backing up too, call Cobb County's emergency dispatch at 770-419-6201.
  • 4
    Find the outside cleanout. A full cleanout means the blockage is toward the street.
  • 5
    Take photos. Before any cleanup, for insurance.
