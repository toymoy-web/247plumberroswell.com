Planned Communities Hitting Their Plumbing Midlife

Johns Creek became a city on December 1, 2006, but most of its houses are older than that. The golf and swim-tennis communities that define it, such as St. Ives, Medlock Bridge and Rivermont, went up largely from the late 1980s into the 2000s.

That timing matters. Houses from those years are now 30 to 35 years old. Many are on their third water heater, their original shutoff valves have not been turned in decades, and the oldest sections can still have polybutylene supply pipe, which was installed across metro Atlanta until about 1995.

Water Made Next Door

Johns Creek is home to the Tom Lowe Atlanta-Fulton County Water Treatment Plant, which treats Chattahoochee River water for Fulton County's system serving Alpharetta, Johns Creek, Milton and Roswell. According to Fulton County's water quality reports, the plant disinfects with sodium hypochlorite and adds lime and orthophosphate to protect pipes from corrosion.

The water tests soft, so the scale problems that plague hard-water cities are rare here. When Johns Creek homeowners ask about treatment, it is usually about taste. Fulton County has explained that seasonal lake turnover can bring an earthy taste, and a carbon filter at the tap handles that better than a softener would.

Neighboring Milton uses the same water but has far more septic systems, while Dunwoody to the southwest runs on DeKalb County's system.

What to Do Before We Arrive in Johns Creek

Gated communities and big houses add a couple of steps.

  • 1
    Close the main or the fixture stop. In many of these homes the main is in the basement or garage where the line enters.
  • 2
    Turn off the right water heater. If there are two, find which one is leaking before shutting off gas or power.
  • 3
    Clear the gate. Leave a gate code or notify the guardhouse so the plumber is not held at the entrance.
  • 4
    Photograph the damage. Insurers usually want pictures before cleanup begins.
  • 5
    Note the build year. It tells the plumber what pipe material to expect.
