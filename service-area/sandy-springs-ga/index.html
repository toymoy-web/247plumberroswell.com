Sandy Springs, Georgia

Your Water Comes From Atlanta, Your Sewer From Fulton County

Sandy Springs is one of the few North Fulton cities where the water does not come from Fulton County. The City of Sandy Springs states that the City of Atlanta's Department of Watershed Management provides water service and handles billing for both water and sewer, while Fulton County maintains the sewer system itself.

The arrangement predates the city. Sandy Springs has bought water from Atlanta since before it incorporated in 2005, delivered from Atlanta's Hemphill plant on 17th Street, and local reporting has described a long dispute over the roughly 21 percent surcharge charged to customers outside Atlanta's city limits.

For a homeowner, the practical result is three different phone numbers. A main break in the street is Atlanta's. A blocked public sewer main is Fulton County's. Anything between your house and those mains is yours, and that is where Roswell 24/7 Plumbing comes in, across Sandy Springs in 30328, 30350, 30342 and 30327, 24 hours a day.

Local Conditions

Older Homes, River Hills and Main Breaks

Much of Sandy Springs' housing went up in the decades after World War II, with plenty of newer infill mixed in. Houses from the 1950s through the 1970s are the ones most likely to have galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains or original clay laterals, and those are the systems that produce slow leaks, rusty water and root-choked sewer lines.

The hills running down to the Chattahoochee add their own pattern. Laterals on steep lots near Riverside Drive and the river parks can be deep at the street end, which affects the cost of any excavation. That makes a camera inspection before deciding between a dig and a trenchless liner especially worthwhile here.

Main breaks happen too. In November 2019, WSB-TV reported a break at Riverside Drive and Blackwater Trail that left about 100 homes without water one morning. When water returns after a break like that, air and sediment can clog faucet aerators and appliance screens. If one faucet runs weak afterward, rinse its aerator before calling anyone.

What to Do Before You Call

These checks tell you whose problem it is.

  • 1
    Ask a neighbor. No water on the whole street means an Atlanta main issue, not your plumbing.
  • 2
    Test the meter. With every tap off, a moving leak indicator means water is leaving on your side.
  • 3
    Check more than one drain. Only the lowest drain backing up suggests your lateral. Several homes backing up suggests the Fulton main.
  • 4
    Rinse aerators after an outage. Sediment after a main repair often looks like a pressure problem.
  • 5
    Find the cleanout. An outside cleanout makes lateral clearing faster and cleaner.
