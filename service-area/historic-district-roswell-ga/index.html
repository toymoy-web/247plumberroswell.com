Historic Roswell

The Oldest Part of the City

The Roswell Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974. The listing covers about 120 acres, roughly bounded by Big Creek, King and Dam streets and the New Marietta Highway. Its structures date from as early as 1837.

The landmarks are well known. Bulloch Hall and Barrington Hall were both built in 1839. Mimosa Hall followed soon after. The Old Bricks, near the mill, were built to house Roswell Mills workers. Roswell Town Square sits at the center of it all.

Around that core, Canton Street has become a line of restaurants and shops, with houses and newer infill homes on the streets nearby. That mix is why this page covers homes and businesses together. A call from a 19th century house and a call from a busy kitchen on the same block are both common here.

Plumbing in a Mixed Historic Neighborhood

Work on older buildings goes better when the plan respects what is already there. Opening plaster walls, moving a vent or changing anything on a street-facing exterior can involve review by the City, so ask the City of Roswell before a repair that alters the outside of a property. Document the condition of walls and floors with photos before work starts, and get the price and the process explained up front.

Restaurants and shops bring a different set of needs. Commercial kitchens send grease into their drain lines, and a backup during service costs money by the hour. Upstairs and downstairs tenants can also share a leak, which makes finding the true source the first job.

Review boards commonly use the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation as their guide, and those standards favor repairing original material over replacing it. In practice that means routing new pipe through closets and floors where possible, and avoiding new holes in visible walls.

For restaurants, a few habits prevent most emergency calls. Scrape plates before they reach the dish machine, keep the grease interceptor on a regular pumping schedule, and do not pour fryer oil down any drain. If a floor drain starts backing up during service, shut off the sinks feeding it and call.

Downtown homes near Barrington Hall and Roswell Town Square are more likely to be on the Roswell Water Utility than on Fulton County water. Your water bill confirms which one serves your address, and that is who you call about the meter or the street side.

For the rest of the city, start at our Roswell plumbing home page. South of downtown, Atlanta Street crosses the Chattahoochee and becomes Roswell Road in Sandy Springs, which we also cover.

Before You Call From an Older Home or Downtown Business

  • 1
    Find the main shutoff. In older houses it may be in a crawl space or cellar. In a shop, it may be in a back room or a shared utility closet.
  • 2
    Tell us if the building is shared. Knowing whether a unit sits above or below another tenant helps trace a leak faster.
  • 3
    Stop sending water to a backed-up drain. In a kitchen, pause the dish machine and sinks feeding the line.
  • 4
    Mention any exterior work. If a repair might touch a street-facing wall or roof, it helps to raise historic review early.
