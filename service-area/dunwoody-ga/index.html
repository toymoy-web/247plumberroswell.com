An Aging Public Grid and Layered Pipes Inside the House

Dunwoody's plumbing story has two layers, one under the street and one inside the walls. Water and sewer come from DeKalb County's Department of Watershed Management, and parts of that system are old. In July 2026, DeKalb said Dunwoody Knoll alone had seen 49 reported water main breaks from 1989 through 2024, caused in large part by pipe installed in the 1950s and 1970s. The county is now replacing about half a mile of main along Spalding Drive and Chamblee Dunwoody Road.

Main breaks and repairs can send pressure surges into houses. The IPC, which Georgia's plumbing code is based on, calls for a pressure-reducing valve where street pressure exceeds 80 psi, and a working regulator is the best protection an older Dunwoody house has against a surge finding its weakest fitting.

Main breaks are DeKalb's to repair. What they do to your house is not. Pressure surges, sediment and air in the lines hit the weakest fitting indoors, and in Dunwoody that fitting is often decades old.

Three Decades of Building, Three Kinds of Pipe

Dunwoody incorporated in 2008, but its neighborhoods were built out long before, mostly from the 1960s into the 1990s. Early ranches and split-levels gave way to two-story colonials, and the Colonial Williamsburg look around Dunwoody Village dates to the shopping center built there in the 1970s. Perimeter Mall opened in 1971 and pulled development toward 30346.

That long build window means one house can hold several generations of plumbing: galvanized steel from the original build, copper from a 1980s kitchen update, polybutylene in an addition, and PEX where something was repaired recently. Each ages differently. Galvanized rusts closed, polybutylene cracks at its fittings, and old copper can develop pinholes.

The practical takeaway is to identify what is actually in the walls before choosing a fix. Sometimes one section needs replacing. Sometimes the answer is a full repipe.

What to Do Before We Arrive in Dunwoody

Steps that limit damage and help the diagnosis.

  • 1
    Close the house main. It stops a burst whether or not the street main is also having problems.
  • 2
    Check with a neighbor. No water on the whole street points to a DeKalb main break.
  • 3
    Turn off the water heater if a hot line burst. Gas knob to Off or its breaker off.
  • 4
    Look at the pipe at the water heater. Galvanized, copper, gray polybutylene or PEX. It tells the plumber what to bring.
  • 5
    Photograph everything. For insurance, before cleanup starts.
