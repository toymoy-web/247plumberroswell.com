Edenwilde

A Swim and Tennis Community in North Roswell

Edenwilde is a north Roswell neighborhood of roughly 390 to 400 brick homes, entered from Edenwilde Road. Neighborhood guides say most homes were built in the 1990s. They are two-story brick and stucco colonials on cul-de-sac streets, and basements and multicar garages are standard.

The community has an eight-lane pool, six lighted tennis courts and a clubhouse. Sweet Apple Park is under a mile away on Crabapple Road, and Crabapple Road also leads south to downtown Roswell.

Edenwilde Drive falls inside Roswell city limits, according to the Census Bureau's address lookup. To the north, the historic Crabapple crossroads belongs to the City of Milton, which has its own page on this site.

Basements, Water Heaters and 30-Year-Old Fixtures

A standard basement shapes much of the plumbing in a house like these. Water heaters often sit downstairs, so a leaking tank can soak finished space before anyone notices. If your basement has a sump pit, the pump is worth testing before a storm. If a basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, it relies on an ejector pump with its own alarm.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) has a separate section for sumps and ejectors, covering sealed basins, venting and check valves, so an ejector replacement is more than dropping in a new pump. The same code calls for a drain pan under a water heater where a leak would cause damage, which describes most finished basements.

Homes built in the 1990s are now about 30 years old. That is roughly when original faucets, fill valves and shutoffs start to wear, and when a first or second water heater reaches the end of its life. None of this means a given house has a problem. It is a sensible list to check.

Before You Call From Edenwilde

  • 1
    Check the basement floor. Water near the heater, the sump pit or a floor drain tells us where to start.
  • 2
    Listen for the sump pump. A pump that hums without moving water, or does not run at all, needs attention before rain.
  • 3
    Find the basement shutoffs. The main and the water heater cold supply are often downstairs.
  • 4
    Test a running toilet. A few drops of food coloring in the tank show a leaking flapper within about 10 minutes.
