Milton, GA

Crabapple, Birmingham and the Long Roads Between Them

Milton sits at the northern tip of Fulton County, bordered by Roswell and Alpharetta to the south, Forsyth County to the east and Cherokee County to the north and west. It became a city on December 1, 2006, but its old crossroads communities, Crabapple, Birmingham and Field's Cross Roads, still shape how people describe where they live.

For an emergency plumber in Milton, GA, the geography matters more than the history. Much of the city is zoned for large agricultural lots, with horse farms, long driveways and homes set far back from the road. That means water service lines that run hundreds of feet from the meter, pipes feeding barns and outbuildings, and houses where the nearest cleanout may be a long walk from the street.

Roswell 24/7 Plumbing covers Milton from the Roswell side, answering emergency calls around the clock and scheduling planned work like line replacements and repipes with free estimates.

Septic or Sewer? Your Bill Tells You

The City of Milton does not provide water or sewer service. Water comes from Fulton County. Sewer, where it exists, also comes from Fulton County, with wastewater from this part of the county treated at the Little River Water Reclamation Facility in Woodstock. The City's own guidance is simple: if you live in Milton and do not pay for sewer service, you are likely on a septic system.

That distinction changes almost every drain call. In a sewered home, a house-wide backup points to the main building drain or the lateral. In a septic home, it often points to a tank that needs pumping or a drainfield that is struggling. Cabling a line that is backing up because the tank is full wastes money, so the first question on a Milton call is always which system you have.

Septic tank maintenance and repair is the homeowner's responsibility under the City's guidance, and connecting a property to sewer or abandoning a tank goes through Fulton County. We clear the drains inside and out to the tank, and drain cleaning on a septic property starts with a tank-level check.

What to Do Before a Hard Freeze on a Milton Property

Georgia cold snaps are short, but outbuildings and long lines are exposed. The IPC, which Georgia's plumbing code is based on, requires water pipe in spaces subject to freezing to be protected, and on a Milton property that often means barns, pump houses and garages.

  • 1
    Drain lines to unheated buildings. Close the shutoff for the barn or workshop and open the faucets there to empty the pipe.
  • 2
    Disconnect every hose. A hose left on a spigot traps water that freezes back into the wall.
  • 3
    Cover exposed pipe. Foam insulation on pipes in crawl spaces, pump houses and garages is inexpensive.
  • 4
    Know where the main is. On a big lot the house shutoff may be far from the meter. Find both before the cold arrives.
  • 5
    Check any backflow assembly. Irrigation assemblies above ground can split. An insulated cover helps.

If a pipe splits anyway, close the main and call (770) 214-4545. Freeze repairs are answered 24/7.
