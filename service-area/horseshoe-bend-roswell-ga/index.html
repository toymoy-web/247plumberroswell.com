Horseshoe Bend

A Golf Community Inside a Bend of the River

Horseshoe Bend takes its name from the Horseshoe Bend Country Club at its core, which overlooks the Chattahoochee River in the southeast part of Roswell. Some homes look out over the river or one of the neighborhood's lakes. Public neighborhood guides count about 1,400 homes and put the typical build year in the mid-1980s.

Most houses are single-family homes in traditional styles, from Colonial-inspired designs to Cape Cods, with some newer farmhouse-style builds. There is also a townhouse community. Children here attend Fulton County Schools such as River Eves Elementary and Centennial High.

The streets around the fairways are inside Roswell city limits. A Census Bureau address check on a street in the neighborhood returns the City of Roswell, which means City of Roswell permits apply to plumbing work that needs one.

What Established Golf-Course Homes Tend to Need

A house built in the mid-1980s has had roughly 40 years of use. Over that time, most homes go through several water heaters, and many have had fixtures, valves and supply lines replaced at least once. The age on the water heater label is usually the first thing worth checking.

Big lots with irrigated lawns come with backflow assemblies near the street and sprinkler lines under the turf. A leak there usually shows up as a higher bill or a soggy patch of grass rather than water in the house.

EPA WaterSense guidelines suggest reading the meter, using no water for two hours, then reading it again to confirm a leak. Mature trees also grow alongside sewer laterals, so a slow main drain is worth a camera look before anyone digs.

East Roswell neighborhoods such as Horseshoe Bend are more likely to be Fulton County water customers. Your water bill confirms it, and that is who you call about the meter.

Before You Call From Horseshoe Bend

  • 1
    Check the water heater label. The manufacture date tells us whether a repair or a replacement makes more sense.
  • 2
    Shut the irrigation valve if the bill jumped. If the leak stops, the problem is in the sprinkler system, not the house.
  • 3
    Note any wet turf. A soggy strip between the meter and the house points to the service line.
  • 4
    Say whether it is a house or a townhome. Shared walls change how a leak is traced and who else needs to know.
