Crawl Space Plumbing

Why So Much Roswell Plumbing Lives Under the Floor

Roswell is hilly. Houses built on sloped lots, especially from the 1960s through the 1990s, often sit on a crawl space or a partial basement rather than a slab. That puts the water supply lines, the drain lines and sometimes the main shutoff in a low, damp space most owners rarely see.

Crawl space plumbing and leak repair covers everything under a raised floor: supply lines, drain and vent piping, cast iron sections, hangers and supports, and the freeze protection that keeps those pipes from splitting in a cold snap. Roswell 24/7 Plumbing works in crawl spaces across Roswell in 30075 and 30076.

The advantage of a crawl space is access. A leak that would mean cutting concrete in a slab home is often a straightforward repair from underneath. The drawback is that leaks can run for weeks before anyone notices.

Raised-floor construction shows up on many of the hillier streets of West Roswell, in neighborhoods such as Wexford and Chickering, and in older homes on the blocks around Bulloch Hall and Mimosa Boulevard, where the lots drop away toward Vickery Creek.

What to Check Before You Call

You can learn a lot from the access door without going in.

  • 1
    Use your nose. A musty or sewage smell near floor vents or the access door points to moisture underneath.
  • 2
    Watch the meter. With all water off, a moving leak indicator means a supply leak somewhere, often under the floor.
  • 3
    Look with a flashlight. From the entrance, look for puddles on the vapor barrier, dripping pipes or insulation hanging down.
  • 4
    Stay out of standing water. Do not crawl in if there is water near wiring, a gas smell or sewage.
  • 5
    Note the date. When the smell started or the bill jumped helps narrow how long the leak has been running.

If sewage is involved or water is actively spraying, close the main and call right away.

For reference, the IPC support table that Georgia's code follows calls for horizontal PVC drain pipe to be supported about every 4 feet and PEX supply tubing about every 32 inches. Drain lines that have dropped between widely spaced hangers are a frequent find. Supply leaks that trace back to polybutylene throughout the house point toward a full repipe.

Freeze Protection Under a Georgia House

Georgia winters are mild most of the time, which is exactly why crawl space pipes get left unprotected. Then a hard freeze arrives. During the December 2022 holiday freeze, Roswell fire crews logged more than 100 burst pipe calls in one day, according to Atlanta News First, and pipes near foundation vents and rim joists are among the most exposed.

The fix is not complicated. Foam insulation on supply lines, especially near vents and outside walls, closed or covered foundation vents during cold snaps, and sealed gaps where pipes pass through the rim joist all help. The IPC, adopted as the basis of Georgia's plumbing code, requires water pipes in spaces subject to freezing to be protected, so this is a code matter as well as common sense.
